# Incumbent analysis: Irish residential pre-submission planning intelligence

**24 September 2026 | Research memo | Scope:** professional assessment of Irish residential schemes, especially 10+ units, immediately before planning submission. This is a public-source desk review, not hands-on testing of paid accounts. Vendor claims are identified as such. “Absent” means not documented in reviewed public material, never proof the feature does not exist.

## Executive assessment

**Your clearest opening is a proposal-specific, evidence-cited pre-submission review for the developer and its planner.** An Irish direct competitor, Planner.ie, advertises approval odds, so the score alone is not a novel category. Precedent.ie, EirePlan, PlanIntel and several report vendors already make precedents, refusal reasons, policy and site constraints easier to obtain. The proposed differentiator is the combination of a **dated, out-of-sample tested refusal probability for a specific 10+ unit scheme; a collaborative risk register with assigned mitigations and planner override; and an exportable decision record**. Your supplied product brief documents these capabilities and the holdout results, but these are **founder-provided, not independently audited**. User-provided *Product & Accuracy Brief* (24 September 2026); [Planner.ie](https://planner.ie/); [Precedent](https://precedent.ie/); [EirePlan pricing](https://eireplan.com/pricing); [PlanIntel](https://planintel.ie/).

**The most serious competitive risk is data/workflow breadth, not whether someone else has a classifier.** Precedent has current zoning over 27 authorities, title boundaries, national sourcing and recent planning refreshes; EirePlan has appeal and commencement context, linked source documents, shared workspaces and an API; both reach research tasks before a submission brief is commissioned. Your current snapshot ends 23 December 2025 and your zoning is limited to four Dublin authorities. These gaps can undermine confidence in a live 2026 decision. [Precedent](https://precedent.ie/); [Precedent pricing](https://precedent.ie/pricing); [EirePlan](https://eireplan.com/); User-provided *Product & Accuracy Brief* (24 September 2026).

**Do not infer weak demand from scarce public AI predictor metrics.** Planning data and site reports are established paid jobs; it remains unproven that a numeric refusal probability improves a professional team's decisions enough to command a recurring subscription. The next evidence is paid pilot conversion, repeated use across a pipeline and a review of cases where the tool changed a decision. [Precedent pricing](https://precedent.ie/pricing); [PlanningAlerts pricing](https://www.planningalerts.ie/pricing); [PrePlanning.ie](https://www.preplanning.ie/).

| Homeowner or agent | Planner.ie, one-off pre-planning reports | Lower price, simpler report; **not** the natural first target for a 10+ unit scheme model |

The actual competitor is often a **planner assembling a bespoke memo from several tools**. Measure the complete work product and approval workflow, not merely whether a platform offers a probability. The best design partner is a developer with a recurring 10+ unit pipeline and a planning consultant who can critique false positives and missing grounds.

## 3. Priority dossiers

### Planner.ie — the direct claim competitor

**Offer:** address-based, one-off Irish approval odds with local comparables, risk tips and recommendations; speaks to homeowners, architects and estate agents, while mentioning developers. €74.99 per report, and its public call to action still says “Join the waitlist.” It advertises “1,000+ users,” without specifying paying customers, repeat usage or measurement period. We found no published time-split residential holdout, calibration, large-scheme results, reason-level evaluation or example of a planner override in the reviewed public page. This is an **unverified public-evidence gap**, not evidence its model cannot do these things. [Planner.ie](https://planner.ie/); [Founder announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/gorannikic_big-update-from-us-at-plannerie-we-activity-7393929707773169664-LbTB).

**Implication:** buy a report when broadly available and give it the **same 10+ unit applications and information cutoff** as your tool. Record numeric predictions, date, input burden, evidence, caveats and actual outputs. Treat its waitlist and user claim as a lead to verify, not traction proof. Target professional repeat workflow before copying homeowner positioning.

### Precedent.ie — the likely toughest Irish research alternative

**Offer:** site sourcing on RZLT parcels, Land Registry title boundaries, zoning, flood/ecology/heritage/subsoil, planning history and extracted refusal themes. The vendor discloses 203,449 decisions, 16,834 refusal reason extracts, register starting in 2021, zoning for 27 authorities and protected-structure coverage for nine. It states no owner names, individual site sale prices or appraisal model. €195/month per login with 12-month minimum; demo and invoicing arranged manually. No public customer or ARR metric located. [Product and coverage](https://precedent.ie/); [Price](https://precedent.ie/pricing).

**What to learn:** transparency about missing layers and refresh dates is part of the product. Their title and current zoning materially beat your current surface for site screening. Your possible edge is the submitted **scheme** as the unit of analysis, a defensible 10+ unit holdout and a planner-owned mitigation register. Demonstrate this side by side on one genuine live case; do not claim that a probability substitutes for the richer land data.

### EirePlan — practitioner workspace and potential data partner

**Offer:** national source-linked planning record; decisions, conditions, council documents, zoning/constraints, site reports and cited assistant. The free tier includes 50 searches and six sites/month; paid plans add higher limits, team sharing and API. It names an integration in production since 2026, which indicates a real deployment but says nothing about paid seats or revenue. Public pricing for paid plans is not posted. Proposal-specific probability is not documented in the reviewed offering. [Product](https://eireplan.com/); [Price](https://eireplan.com/pricing).

**What to learn:** its document register and pooled team use may be what a planning practice values most. Your planning-pack PDF ingestion is therefore a higher priority than generic feature parity. Explore whether cited source data or API partnership can close freshness/coverage gaps economically; check terms, provenance, costs and rights before integration.

### PlanIntel — low-price evidence search

**Offer:** national search, refusal explanations grounded in source sentences, watches, AI chat and €39 site brief. Vendor states 30 authorities indexed (Cork City exception), 493,447 applications and 8,858 document analyses. It explicitly declines to predict an individual decision. This makes it both a substitute for precedent research and a useful positioning contrast. [PlanIntel](https://planintel.ie/); [Pricing](https://planintel.ie/pricing).

**What to learn:** their source-linked refusal sentences raise the bar for explainability. Your top-three refusal-theme coverage (~56% on the supplied refused 10+ unit holdout) must never be presented as a reliable top-one reason forecast. Offer the score as triage and the precedents as evidence, with a named planner checking the site-specific grounds. User-provided *Product & Accuracy Brief* (24 September 2026).

### Pin Point Alerts (PlanningAlerts.ie and PrePlanning.ie) — established workflow substitutes

**Offer:** alerts from planning feeds and paid professional accounts; pre-planning reports gather nearby history, conservation, geology, roads, sales, boundaries and shadow analysis, including a populated AA screen. The report is pitched to council staff, agents and applicants, and business subscriptions are quoted. A €29/year consumer alert plan is **not** a fair price anchor for a development review. Public customers, MAU and ARR are undisclosed. [PlanningAlerts](https://www.planningalerts.ie/pricing); [PrePlanning](https://www.preplanning.ie/).

**What to learn:** a professional values packaged spatial evidence even without AI. Test whether your report saves material *planner time* after their existing tools are in place, rather than comparing your workflow to raw council portals.

### UK analogues — scale and customer experience

LandTech spans sourcing, constraints, appraisal, funding and a cited AI assistant, and claims 5,000+ UK developer users/customers without defining monthly activity. Searchland spans ownership, outreach, planning and appraisal and names many customer cases, including a 100+ person land team. Nimbus focuses on finding, assessing and contacting owners. These are not Irish launch incumbents, but set expectations for polished data, onboarding, ownership tools and team adoption. [LandTech](https://land.tech/); [Searchland](https://searchland.co.uk/); [Nimbus](https://www.nimbusmaps.co.uk/commercial-developer).

Planda markets UK prediction reports and a 99% outcome accuracy claim. **Never compare its “accuracy” to your AUC**: accuracy at an unspecified threshold and base rate is a different metric, and the page does not establish a comparable holdout. Its page simultaneously claims 350+ and 3,000+ reports daily, so treat its volume as unverified. Archistar shows an alternative path: check full drawings against encoded policy rather than infer outcomes from a small form. Your planned document ingestion should explore this complement, while acknowledging that code compliance does not settle discretionary planning decisions. [Planda](https://www.planda.co.uk/); [Planda claim](https://www.planda.co.uk/council-reports); [Archistar/ICC](https://www.iccsafe.org/about/periodicals-and-newsroom/international-code-council-collaborates-with-archistar-to-modernize-permitting-and-accelerate-housing-development/).

## 5. Uptake, revenue and market reality

**Do not equate indexed applications, generated reports, testimonials, social followers, or “trusted by” logos with monthly active paying users.** Vendors generally do not publish MAU or ARR. Publicly surfaced proxies here are Planner.ie’s vendor claim of 1,000+ users despite waitlist messaging; LandTech’s 5,000+ UK developers; Vizlegal’s hundreds of barristers weekly (across legal products); Searchland’s named 100+ person team; and EirePlan’s named integration. Each measures something different. [Planner.ie](https://planner.ie/); [LandTech](https://land.tech/); [Vizlegal](https://www.vizlegal.com/blog/from-planning-application-to-judicial-review-how-vizlegal-covers-the-full-picture/); [Searchland](https://searchland.co.uk/); [EirePlan](https://eireplan.com/).

**ARR arithmetic, not estimates of competitors:** Precedent’s public list contract of €195 × 12 = **€2,340 per login per year**. Fifty comparable full-price logins would be €117,000 in annualised contracted revenue; 100 would be €234,000, *before* discounts, churn, VAT and service costs. No evidence says Precedent has those seat counts. SiteSight’s Pro list price implies €359.88 per seat/year if held 12 months; its Business price €719.88. Planner.ie’s €74.99 is transaction revenue, not ARR, and a €39 PlanIntel brief cannot be treated as a subscription. [Precedent price](https://precedent.ie/pricing); [SiteSight price](https://www.sitesight.ie/pricing); [Planner.ie](https://planner.ie/); [PlanIntel](https://planintel.ie/).

**Addressable-use warning:** Your founder-provided 2024–25 holdout contains **464 decided 10+ unit applications** (232/year on average), an outcome cohort, not a count of unique prospective purchasers or all schemes screened before submission. Selling one €75 report per eventual decision cannot support a large standalone Irish business at that volume (~€17,400/year before any adjustments), but buyers may screen multiple schemes per submitted one, buy team subscriptions, and use the tool for 2–9 units or other geographies. Establish actual sites screened per buyer per year before building a TAM model. User-provided *Product & Accuracy Brief* (24 September 2026); [Planner.ie price](https://planner.ie/).

## 6. Your position, honestly scored

**Founder-provided model evidence:** trained on 183,439 pre-2024 applications; frozen 2024–25 all-residential holdout n=35,713 has AUC 0.792 and Brier 0.0901. For 10+ units, n=464, AUC 0.7823, AP 0.5298 and Brier 0.1247; the top decile refusal frequency is 63.8% versus 0% in the bottom decile, with wide CIs. The user brief states these results are out of sample with submission-time features. An external buyer should receive the protocol and a reproducible audit artifact; the web review does not independently validate the metrics. The refusal *ground* capability is weaker: top three surfaced themes cover 55.6% of refused large schemes in the stated test. User-provided *Product & Accuracy Brief* (24 September 2026).

## 7. Recommended wedge and commercial test

**Wedge:** “Pre-submission risk review for Irish residential schemes of 10+ units, prepared for developer and planner sign-off.” Sell **speed, prioritisation and defensible documentation**. State that the planner owns the recommendation. The user’s credentials may help win a meeting, but the first slide should show a real site, an issue found, evidence source, action owner, time saved and what the tool could not verify.

**Pilot design (6–8 weeks, proposed experiment, not market fact):** Recruit 5–8 developers or planning practices via warm introductions. Ask each for two previously decided schemes and two live schemes. On decided schemes, reconstruct the information available immediately before submission; run blind reviews and compare output against their original memo. On live schemes, time the incumbent research process and your assisted workflow, count *verified* issues found, false alarms, changes to drawings/brief, planner edits, and whether they would use it again. Ask for a paid pilot before discussing a broad rollout. Record the denominator: approached, meeting, activated, repeated, paid, renewed. Do not claim refusal avoidance without a controlled counterfactual.

**Pricing hypothesis to test:** per-project paid review for infrequent teams and annual team licence for repeat developers/planners; quote enterprise where they need provenance, private data, audit/export and support. Use €39–€75 one-off Irish report offers and €195/login/month research licence as **anchors for interview questions**, not a dictated price. Price against *documented time saved and decision value*. [PlanIntel](https://planintel.ie/); [Planner.ie](https://planner.ie/); [Precedent](https://precedent.ie/pricing).

**Product order:** 1) live data freshness/Cork integrity and authority-wide zoning, 2) PDF plan-pack ingestion with exact citations, 3) same-site and paraphrase robustness, 4) shared planner review and export, 5) only then extra land sourcing or valuation. A large horizontal feature set can be copied; trustworthy data, calibrated claims and repeat usage take longer to establish. The ordering is an analytical recommendation grounded in the brief’s stated limitations and the rivals’ published strengths. User-provided *Product & Accuracy Brief* (24 September 2026); [Precedent](https://precedent.ie/); [EirePlan](https://eireplan.com/).
